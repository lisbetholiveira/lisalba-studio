# Quality Principles and Responsible AI

## 1. Human accountability

AI supports exploration and production; it does not replace responsibility. A human reviews consequential creative decisions and authorises external delivery or publication.

## 2. Truthful representation

- Do not invent clients, commissions, testimonials, results or performance metrics.
- Label conceptual, speculative and AI-generated work clearly where context requires it.
- Distinguish verified facts from hypotheses, creative interpretation and unknowns.

## 3. Brief-led quality

Quality is measured against the objective, audience, context and approved creative direction—not against novelty alone. Every output should have a reason to exist within the system.

## 4. Visual craft

Review composition, hierarchy, typography, colour, materiality, anatomy, continuity, artefacts, resolution and format. Selective curation is part of production, not an optional final step.

## 5. Consistency with room for variation

A visual system should remain recognisable across channels while allowing format-specific adaptation. Variations must preserve the defining concept and brand signals.

## 6. Privacy and confidentiality

- Use only the information needed for the task.
- Keep credentials, personal data, private prompts and confidential client material out of public systems.
- Sanitise case studies and documentation before publication.

## 7. Rights-aware sourcing

- Use assets with an appropriate licence, permission or documented basis.
- Respect trademarks, publicity rights and contractual restrictions.
- Avoid presenting a synthetic person or voice as a real endorsement.

## 8. Fairness and dignity

Review outputs for stereotypes, objectification and harmful or exclusionary representation. Take particular care with identity, health, age, body image and culturally sensitive contexts.

## 9. Accessibility and usability

Consider contrast, legibility, captions, alternative text, pacing and device context. A visually strong asset should still be understandable and usable by its intended audience.

## 10. Proportionate evidence

Claims—especially in health, wellness and product performance—must be supported and phrased in proportion to the available evidence. Creative language must not turn uncertainty into certainty.

## 11. Traceable iteration

Keep enough version and review context to explain why an output was selected, revised or rejected. Documentation should support learning without exposing sensitive material.

## 12. Release discipline

Internal approval, a preview or a generated draft is not publication. Release requires a final check of content, rights, format, destination and human authorisation.

## Review checklist

Before release, confirm:

- The output matches the approved brief and direction.
- Names, facts, claims, dates and links are accurate.
- Conceptual or AI-generated material is represented transparently.
- Rights, consent, privacy and confidentiality requirements are satisfied.
- No sensitive information or credentials are present.
- The asset works in its intended format and context.
- Accessibility requirements have been considered.
- A human has approved the release.
